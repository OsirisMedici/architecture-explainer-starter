# Explanation boundaries

The starter communicates a design idea. It does not calculate building performance.

| Example | What the diagram explains | What needs separate project analysis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Natural ventilation | A connected low-to-high opening and density differences can drive airflow; reversing the temperature difference can reverse the direction. | Wind, pressure, effective opening area, airflow rate, moisture, comfort, pollutants and fire strategy. |
| Sun protection | A fixed overhang can intercept one high-angle ray while allowing a lower ray below it. | Latitude, orientation, date, time, surrounding obstructions, actual solar altitude and azimuth, energy and daylight results. |
| Building layers | The spatial relationship between floor plates and the roof. | Structure, connections, sequencing and construction safety. |
| Compare options | A geometric difference and its stated design tradeoff. | Cost, area schedules, code compliance, lighting, thermal performance and whether either option is preferable. |

The sample temperature values and rays are teaching examples. Retain the illustrative label. If the client provides analysis, identify its source, revision, assumptions and scope; do not relabel the whole starter as a validated simulation.

Primary references used for the examples:

- [WHO, Understanding natural ventilation](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK143285/).
- [US Department of Energy, Natural Ventilation and Cooling](https://bsesc.energy.gov/energy-basics/natural-ventilation-and-cooling).
- [Pacific Northwest National Laboratory, Shading and Solar Control](https://basc.pnnl.gov/resource-guides/shading-and-solar-control-windows-and-skylights).
